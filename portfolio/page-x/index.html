Digital Design for Social Causes

This collection showcases my digital design work within the advocacy space. Below, I provide context for each asset to highlight the social impact and design strategy behind these projects.

'Know Your Rights' Zine

Developed in collaboration with the Awesome Foundation of Raleigh-Durham and the Puffin Foundation, this project educates residents on their constitutional rights during ICE encounters, emphasizing accessible, actionable information.

'Nice People Use Drugs Too' Poster

Created for Project Weber/Renew, this poster serves a dual purpose: fundraising and conveying vital harm-reduction messaging to support drug-positive health policies.

Gay Pride Coloring Book

A collaboration with the LGBT Center, this zine illustrates key moments in queer history—from the AIDS quilt to Holocaust victims—engaging audiences through educational, visual storytelling.

'Zine for Good' Cover

This cover design promotes 'Zine for Good,' a research-driven project highlighting design work that serves the public interest. It helps reframe social advocacy as a viable and powerful career path for designers.

Narcan Container Stickers

In partnership with COBRE and Brown University Health, these stickers were designed for Narcan containers. By tailoring visuals and messaging, the campaign effectively engaged diverse audiences, including users, caretakers, and outreach teams.

Portfolio Navigation GIF

This animated GIF demonstrates my technical proficiency in motion design and user experience. It serves as an intuitive navigation tool for the project's framework within my professional portfolio.

'Mexicanitas' Flyer

This project showcases my commercial graphic design work, demonstrating my ability to produce cohesive assets that function effectively across both print and digital platforms.

Food Not Bombs Zines

These zines were designed for the Providence/Brown chapters of Food Not Bombs to support their mutual aid efforts. The 'Zines for Fun' initiative created engaging, playful stories to foster community and joy, while the 'Zines for Recruitment' handbook utilized recognizable imagery to help student organizers recruit and manage food redistribution efforts effectively.
